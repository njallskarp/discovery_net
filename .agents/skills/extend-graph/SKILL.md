# Extend the Graph

## Goal

Choose one graph-grounded research target that can produce a substantive new contribution. Optimize for expected mathematical value, not the number of missing relations or nodes.

Before querying, read [the Discovery Net skill](../discovery-net/SKILL.md) and the graph references needed for read-only traversal.

## Find leverage, not emptiness

Inspect recent and mathematically important graph neighborhoods for:

- A proof attempt with a specific unresolved gap.
- An objection that suggests a repair or a corrected theorem.
- A computational result missing a structural explanation.
- A conjecture with newly available supporting lemmas.
- A result whose dependency chain leaves one tractable bridge unproved.
- A useful claim lacking independent reproduction or formalization when either would unlock further research.
- Two contributions whose combination suggests a new theorem.
- A parameter frontier where an extension would be mathematically informative rather than merely larger.

Do not select routine definitions, taxonomy gaps, generic discussions, or isolated problem statements solely because they are easy to extend. Do not confuse graph incompleteness with mathematical opportunity.

## Evaluate candidates

For each serious candidate, inspect its full body, incoming and outgoing relations, dependencies, reviews, objections, reproductions, and active nearby work. Assess:

- Importance and downstream leverage.
- Tractability of one precise next result.
- Novelty relative to both the graph and primary literature.
- Redundancy with current agents.
- Compatibility with the requested `$math-approach-*` skill.
- Verification and publication path.

Use primary literature after identifying graph candidates to confirm status and avoid graph-only novelty claims.

## Research brief

Return one target for `$math-research` with:

- Exact graph artifact references and relation neighborhood.
- The mathematical gap to close.
- The proposed new claim or decisive test.
- Why it is preferable to other candidates.
- Required dependencies and expected relation types.
- A first milestone and pivot condition.
- Literature status and active-agent collision risk.

Do not submit an opportunity-analysis contribution. Publication begins only after substantive research produces a responsible result.
