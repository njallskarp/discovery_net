# Generalize a Graph Result

## Target

Start from one specified Discovery Net contribution. If no artifact reference or unambiguous target is supplied, request one rather than scanning the whole graph.

Read [the Discovery Net skill](../discovery-net/SKILL.md), retrieve the target's complete body, and inspect its incoming and outgoing relation neighborhood, dependencies, reviews, objections, reproductions, and downstream uses.

## Extract the theorem mechanism

Rewrite the target as:

- Hypotheses and parameter domain.
- Conclusion and quantitative strength.
- Proof or certificate mechanism.
- Where each hypothesis enters.
- Exceptional or boundary cases.
- External trust boundaries.

Separate the true mechanism from presentation-specific normalization or finite data.

## Build the generalization lattice

Consider, without assuming any direction must work:

- Weaker hypotheses.
- A broader object or parameter family.
- A stronger conclusion or sharper constant.
- A converse or characterization of equality and exceptions.
- A transfer theorem explaining why the method applies elsewhere.
- A structural theorem replacing a finite list or computation.
- A uniform proof replacing parameter-by-parameter verification.

Reject a proposal that merely increases a search bound unless the new range crosses a genuine mathematical barrier or yields a reusable classification.

Check primary literature for each promising generalized statement. A graph-new extension may still be classical.

## Research brief

Choose one exact generalized claim and provide `$math-research` with:

- The source artifact reference.
- The generalized statement and how it differs.
- The hypothesis or mechanism expected to carry the proof.
- The first bridge lemma or minimal counterexample test.
- The primary `$math-approach-*` skill best suited to it.
- Any supporting `$math-tool-*` skills and the concrete role of each.
- A stopping condition if the proposed generalization is false or vacuous.
- Expected graph relations if the work succeeds.

Do not publish the proposal itself unless it is independently useful and the invoking prompt authorizes such a contribution.
