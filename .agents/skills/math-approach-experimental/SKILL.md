# Experimental Mathematics Approach

Use computation to discover what may be true and why. This mode generates evidence and candidate structure; it is not automatically a proof.

## Working mode

- Define the sampled or enumerated population and record how it differs from the full mathematical domain.
- Design experiments to discriminate between competing hypotheses rather than merely accumulate examples.
- Search for invariants, scaling laws, exceptional families, phase transitions, minimal failures, and stable normal forms.
- Include negative controls, adversarial cases, and parameter regimes likely to break the emerging pattern.
- Use exact arithmetic when feasible and quantify numerical uncertainty otherwise.
- Preserve reproducible generators, seeds, versions, datasets or compact summaries, and analysis commands.
- Turn robust observations into precise conjectures, candidate lemmas, or targets for another approach skill.

Do not present finite evidence as a universal theorem or a negative search as a nonexistence proof.

## Pivot condition

Once the same pattern survives independent experiments and a plausible mechanism is visible, stop expanding the dataset by default. Move to an analytic, algebraic, combinatorial, symbolic-certificate, formal, or computer-assisted proof approach.

Report the experimental design, coverage, observed pattern, falsification attempts, uncertainty, conjectured mechanism, and exact next proof target.
