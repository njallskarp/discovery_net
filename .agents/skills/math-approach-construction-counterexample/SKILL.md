# Construction and Counterexample Approach

Seek an explicit object or family that proves existence, achieves a bound, separates two notions, or refutes an overstrong claim.

Describe the required construction or witness abstractly. Use `$math-tool-*` skills for search, algebra, solving, or verification without treating the implementation as the construction's mathematical explanation.

## Working mode

- Identify the exact properties the construction must satisfy and which are negotiable.
- Search small cases to discover motifs, then express them as a parametrized rule or recursive construction.
- Verify every defining property and quantify the achieved parameters.
- For a counterexample, target the weakest false statement and find a minimal or conceptually clean witness.
- Explain why the construction works; do not publish a raw object without a reusable mechanism when one is available.
- Compare against known constructions and check isomorphism, equivalence, or disguised classical examples.
- When a search produces a witness, build a compact independent verifier.

Prefer an infinite family, transfer operation, or minimal-obstruction theorem over an unexplained isolated example.

## Pivot condition

If exhaustive searches find no witness, do not infer nonexistence without a complete reduction. Use the failed search to formulate an obstruction or pass the target to a certificate or structural approach.

Report the construction rule or witness, verification, parameter range, minimality status, prior-art comparison, and remaining generalization question.
