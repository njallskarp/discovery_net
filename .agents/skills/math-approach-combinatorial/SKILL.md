# Combinatorial Approach

Seek a finite structural explanation, not merely a finite enumeration.

## Working mode

- Identify the objects, admissible operations, minimal obstructions, and natural statistics.
- Try injections, bijections, double counting, compression, exchange arguments, recurrences, and canonical decompositions.
- Determine whether local constraints force a global structure or whether exceptional configurations can be classified.
- Track labels, automorphisms, multiplicities, and quotient conventions exactly.
- Test conjectured recurrences and extremal forms on small cases, then prove why they persist.
- Treat a computed table as evidence for a structural claim, not as its explanation.

Prefer a characterization, recurrence, forbidden configuration theorem, or extremal principle over another isolated count.

## Pivot condition

If the structural conjecture fails, find the smallest counterexample and extract the missing statistic or obstruction that distinguishes it.

Report the combinatorial mechanism, counting conventions, exceptional family, and the precise role of any enumeration.
